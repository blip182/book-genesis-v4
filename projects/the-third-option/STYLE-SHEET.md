# Style Sheet · The Third Option

*The book-specific rules. Jena's full voice system (voice map, professional voice, hard nos, writing samples) is the parent document set and always wins on conflict. This sheet translates it for a 50,000-word method book and adds rules the book itself created.*

---

## Which voice

Professional voice, book-length register. Short, sharp, systems-minded, direct. Someone who thought hard before speaking. The faith and personal voice does not appear in this book; the book stays secular and broad on purpose. Faith gets its own branch book later.

The formula still applies, in sequence: **Michelle's brain** (is this the actually-right take?), **Arvin's mouth** (does it sound like a person talking? build long, land short), **Travis's filter** (if it could have come from a corporate deck, gut it).

## The never-bend rules (inherited)

- No em dashes. Ever. Periods, commas, or rewrite.
- No "the problem isn't X, it's actually Y" setups. Say what it is. (Watch this one hard: the false-binary premise tempts this framing constantly. State the two doors as the reader lives them, don't build fake wrong answers to knock down.)
- No throat-clearing openers. No "In today's fast-paced world."
- No tidy-bow closings. No "That's the whole thing." Last lines land on their own.
- One idea per sentence. When the real point arrives, it gets its own short sentence.
- Clean grammar, contractions encouraged.
- No corporate jargon: leverage (as a verb), synergy, ecosystem, pivot, robust, unlock/unleash potential.
- Banned phrases: "have it all," manifestation hand-waving, "queen" as filler.
- No hedging that isn't earned. "I could be wrong" only when genuinely held.
- Honest over flattering, everywhere, including how the book talks to the reader.

## The book's own load-bearing rules

1. **Prescribe the method, never the life.** The stages are categories and mindsets. The book never says go to college or don't, hustle or rest, career or hobby. It says: here's how to know which one fits. Every chapter gets checked against this before it's called done.
2. **Own the stance, then leave room.** Jena states what she thinks plainly, makes clear it fits her priorities, and gives the reader space to find their own. Never define someone else's success.
3. **Story is evidence, not memoir.** Every story earns its place by proving a mechanism. A story that's merely good goes to a branch book. (Anti-balloon rule.)
4. **Persistence over perfection is the thesis, and the prose has to model it.** No lesson-style stories that pretend she has it all figured out. The contracts-season chapter exists because she failed at her own method first. Never glamorize the hard parts as easy.
5. **Strength-level framing only.** Neurodivergence and grit appear as she publicly owns them: the wiring as an edge it took years to see, real responsibility carried young. No clinical detail, no leading with trauma or diagnosis as identity, ever.
6. **No fake authority.** One or two curated outside anchors per chapter, then the method carries it. Don't dress the book in research apparatus it doesn't need, and don't make claims the anchors can't hold.

## Sustaining the voice at 4,500 words

The professional voice is calibrated for post-length pieces. At chapter length:

- **The teaching structure is the chapter architecture:** a framework for the thinker, a story for the feeler, a visual or path metaphor for the visual learner. A keynote needs all three; so does a chapter.
- Paragraphs 2 to 4 sentences, with room to run longer when a thought needs it. Single-sentence lines are a landing device, not a default. At book length, staccato everywhere reads as exhausting instead of punchy. Spend the short line where it counts.
- Build with longer sentences, land with a short one. Close sections on a challenge or a real question, not a bow.
- Second person as direct address, not lecture. One reader, talked to, not an audience talked at.
- Metaphors come from systems, paths, and building. Not alchemy, not war, not sports cliché. (The gym stories are literal, not metaphor.)
- Redefining lazy words on her own terms (success, readiness, decisiveness, efficiency, winning) is a signature move and scales well to chapter theses.

Politics, abortion, LGBTQ topics, other religions, war. Pride in the military family may be visible; opinions are not, and the door stays closed to others inserting theirs.

## The litmus test, book edition

Read a passage back and ask: does this sound like a systems thinker who heard the reader, thought about it honestly, and told them what she actually thinks? Or like a self-help book performing a brand? If it's the second, strip it back. Less imitation, more inhabitation.

## Sign-off

Draft freely. Jena engineers and approves. Nothing is final, published, or called done without her sign-off. Never produce something she'd have to disown.
