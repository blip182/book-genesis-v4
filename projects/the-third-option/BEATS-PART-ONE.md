## Chapter 1 · Break the Cycle

**Promise:** The menu you were handed is not the menu.
**Mechanism:** Defaults are cycles, and both doors of the binary are scripts. Breaking the cycle is declining to treat a two-option menu as final. It is not rebellion and it is not wreckage.
**Chapter metaphor (for the visual learner):** the map. The one you were handed shows two roads. The territory has more, and paths can be paved where the map shows nothing. This is the book's home metaphor family (paths, systems, building) and it connects to her own line: pave your own path.
**Named tool (candidate, Jena to confirm):** the recurring question "Who drew this map?" plus three markers of a default wearing a decision costume.

## Chapter 2 · Know Your Core

**Promise:** You can't choose what fits until you know what it has to fit.
**Mechanism:** Knowing your core has two halves: seeing it clearly (often a years-long journey, and that's normal) and amplifying it on purpose, using what's unusual about you as the foundation instead of sanding it down.
**Chapter metaphor:** the foundation, with the accountability spelled out. You don't get to pour a different foundation, but everything after that is on the builder: surveying it honestly, building to what's actually there, owning the result. Buildings crack when the builder ignores the foundation, and ignoring it is a builder's choice. Knowing your core is the survey, and doing the survey is an act of accountability, not self-acceptance as a stopping point.
**Named tool:** the Fit Audit, layer one: the reader authors their own definition of fit before any choosing tools arrive. From here forward the method only audits against the reader's own criteria.

## Chapter 3 · Choose What Fits ← TEST CHAPTER

**Promise:** Yes and no are not your only answers. Almost nothing is take-it-or-leave-it.
**Mechanism:** The counter-offer. Never a raw yes, never a raw no: pause, price it, counter with what you could do and when. Choosing what fits is active negotiation with every opportunity, and the yes-to-everything and no-to-everything seasons are the two doors again, inside a single decision.
**Chapter metaphor:** the offer on the table. Nobody else can see that it's a draft.
**Named tool (candidate, Jena to confirm):** the counter-offer rule, taught as three moves: pause, price it, counter.
