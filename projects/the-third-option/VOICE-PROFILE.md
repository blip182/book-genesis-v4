# Jena Crossland — Voice Profile

A portable guide to how I write, so I sound like myself everywhere: the book, the blog, posts, anywhere.

## How to use this

- **As my own editing pass:** read a draft against the checklist at the bottom. Fix what trips it.
- **As an AI editor prompt:** paste this whole file in and say: *"Edit this to sound like me. Follow my voice profile exactly. Preserve my facts, my stories, and my meaning. Don't add anything I didn't say, and don't smooth me into sounding like everyone else."*

One rule sits above all the others: **when in doubt, say it plainer.** If a line is trying to sound impressive, cut it back until it just sounds true.

---

## My register (what I sound like)

- **Plain, declarative sentences.** I say the thing. I don't decorate it.
- **First person, talking to one reader** as "you." Direct, warm, never salesy.
- **Understated.** I undersell. I would rather say less than oversell. If anything, I make my wins smaller than they were.
- **Honest about hard things without drama.** No pity party, no superhero story. I name a cost plainly and move on. Struggle lives at "strength altitude" — real, but not milked.
- **Comfortable with uncertainty.** I'll admit I didn't have it figured out. "I won't pretend I did." "At the time I didn't even know what the door would be."
- **Specific.** I lead with a real moment — a day, a room, a thing that actually happened — not a general claim.
- **I never prescribe.** I show my life and the method. I never tell you that you *need* to do X, Y, Z. The reader keeps the pen. My favorite version of a point hands the decision back to the reader.

## What I never do (these make it stop sounding like me)

- **No em dashes. Ever.** Use a period, a comma, or restructure the sentence.
- **No "it's not X, it's Y" reversals as a crutch.** ("Readiness is not the prerequisite. Willingness is.") One deliberate one in a whole piece, maybe. Otherwise just say Y.
- **No significance-markers.** Never write a sentence whose only job is to tell the reader what a moment means or that I'm being careful. Banned: "that's the part that got me," "here's the thing," "and that's the point," "I want to point that out," "I want to be careful about how I tell this." If it matters, it already shows. Delete the label.
- **No self-praise or achievement language.** Not "excelled," not "I claimed it," not "the room knew what was mine." I undersell. Plain outcome only: "I felt like I was doing it well."
- **No ownership or credit framing.** Not "it's mine, not hers." I state the thing in first person and let the ownership be implied. Grabbing for credit reads small.
- **No over-explaining or over-reassuring.** Don't defend against an accusation nobody made. Don't restate the point in new clothes. Say it once, where it lands hardest.
- **No flourishes:** no rule-of-three lists for rhythm ("fast, reliable, and scalable"), no rhetorical Q&A drumrolls ("The result? Everything changed."), no "here's the kicker," no hedge stacks ("it's worth noting," "arguably"), no grandiosity ("pivotal moment," "define the next era").
- **No tidy invented anecdotes.** Real specifics or none. If I don't have a real example, I speak to the reader directly instead of inventing a character.

## Themes that should echo across everything I write

These are mine. When they show up, the blog and the book feel like one person:

- **Self-authorship.** You choose your own labels, your own scoreboard, your own definition of a good life. The only one with authority is the one you'd pick on purpose.
- **"Who drew this map?"** Question the defaults. Most "take it or leave it" choices are just a formatting trick.
- **Choose, build, own** — and then choose again.
- **Joy can be a metric you set the scale for.** You're allowed to invest in something purely because it brings you joy, with no deliverable required to justify it.
- **Willingness over readiness.** You don't have to be ready. You have to be willing.
- **Freedom to explore and let go** without it being a failure story.

## Before / after (my actual edits — this is the calibration)

**Killing the reversal crutch**
- Before: *"Readiness is not the prerequisite. Willingness is."*
- After: *"Readiness is not a prerequisite to producing meaningful work. What stands out more is the willingness. I see more growth with a willingness to learn, ask questions, and even fail."*

**Cutting significance-markers (just delete them)**
- Before: *"Michelle became a mentor of mine, and I want to be careful about how I tell this, because the easy version hands her the whole transformation…"*
- After: *"Michelle became a mentor and a friend. She challenges me with sharp questions and gives me a safe space to explore the answers for myself."*

**Dropping credit/ownership grabbing**
- Before: *"That's where I landed on the thing I carried out of it, and it's mine and not hers: I get to choose the label I wear."*
- After: *"I get to choose the label I wear, and the one I choose is the only one with any real authority over how I live."*

**Not describing others when I mean the reader**
- Before: *"The contractor who finally hits the revenue milestone she's chased for three years and feels the flatness arrive…"*
- After: *"You don't have to recognize your life in mine for this to be about you. Maybe you finally hit a number you'd chased for years and felt the flatness arrive before the congratulations did."*

**Reframing off the defensive, onto the real point**
- Before: *"I could tell you those hobbies don't make me a dabbler… that history doesn't prove I can't commit."*
- After: *"The freedom to invest in something purely because it brought me joy… Joy is allowed to be the whole reason. It can be a metric of its own, and you are the one who sets its scale."*

**Putting the reader in the room instead of handing them the lesson**
- Before (summary): *"a direct, vulnerable conversation that my role came out stronger for."*
- After (scene): *"I went back to the leader who had signed the original contract… Not a status update. The real thing… I had my termination clause in mind the whole time I spoke."*

## The 60-second checklist

Run a draft against this. Any "no" is a fix.

- [ ] Zero em dashes.
- [ ] No "it's not X, it's Y" (unless it's the one deliberate beat of the whole piece).
- [ ] No sentence that only announces what a moment means or that I'm being careful. Cut it.
- [ ] No self-praise. Wins are undersold, not oversold.
- [ ] Not defending against an accusation nobody made. Not restating the point.
- [ ] Opens on / uses a real, specific thing — a day, a room, a number — not a generic claim.
- [ ] Never tells the reader what they must do. The decision stays theirs.
- [ ] Sentence lengths vary. Not a drumbeat of short punchy fragments.
- [ ] Every sentence would sound natural said out loud, plainly.
- [ ] If I invented a person or an example to make a point, I replaced it with a real one or spoke to the reader directly instead.
