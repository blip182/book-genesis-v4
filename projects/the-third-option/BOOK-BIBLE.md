## 1. The Method

**The spine:** Choose → Build → Own. Three stages, three tools, one loop.

| Stage | Tool | Full name | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Choose** | Strategy | Freedom of Choice | Getting free enough to pick your own path |
| **Build** | Creativity | Desire to Build | Making the life real through discovery and perseverance |
| **Own** | Operations | Ability to Own | Holding what you built and keeping it better |

**The mechanisms under each (the how-layer):**
- **Choose:** break the cycle · know and amplify your core · choose the path that fits your needs
- **Build:** the discovery process · dig into your true interests · perseverance over comfort
- **Own:** accountability to what you built · the drive to keep bettering it

**It's a method, not a theory or a framework.** A theory is one belief you adopt. This is a sequence you move through and re-run. "Framework" felt clinical, so the public word is *method*, and the brand does the structural signaling instead.

**The stages are categories and mindsets, not instructions.** The method never tells the reader to go to college or skip it, to hustle or to rest. It tells them how to know which one fits. This is a load-bearing rule for every chapter (see STYLE-SHEET.md, "Prescribe the method, never the life").

## 2. Brand Positioning

**Umbrella:** the third option, under the wider "Architecture of" brand. The umbrella is the philosophy; the method is how you live it.

**Essence line:** "I'm not perfect. I'm just willing to stick with things long enough to make it happen." Persistence over perfection.

**The promise underneath it:** A life that fits isn't clean or cookie-cutter. It's chosen. "Fits" is yours to define, loud and messy or calm and consistent, and the method serves both. Your personality shows on purpose; it doesn't get sanded down.

**UVP:** Agency as an operating discipline, not a mindset. A repeatable build-and-own loop from an operator's brain. Strategy, creativity, and operations together is the edge no one else in this space has.

**Problem the book solves:** The false binary. People think they have two doors, comply and resent it or blow it all up, and no one hands them a method for the third path.

**Audience:** The capable person stuck between those two bad options who wants agency without wrecking their life. Secular and broad.

**The reader, made specific (v1.1):** anyone living their life waiting for the good part. Maybe they clock into a job they hate. Maybe they force friend groups they don't fit with. Maybe they're a stay-at-home mom who knows she has drive for something of her own, and it's up to her to decide what that is. A career or a hobby, the book doesn't care which. "Waiting for the good part" is candidate intro language.

**Tangible takeaway:** By the end, readers can run Choose, Build, Own on their own situation, and they leave with a self-audit tool (the Fit Audit).

## 3. The Book · Strategy & Specs

**Sequencing decision:** Write the lean method-first book *first* to establish the method cleanly, partly to claim the language before someone else does. Then run a launch-phase campaign that slowly releases sneak peeks. The other books are tabled, not killed.

**Trunk and branches:** This book is the pure, domain-agnostic method (the trunk). The branches are the later books where the method shows up in specific domains. Jena has named three: faith, the ops career, and the journey of taking off the mask. Sequencing, not loss.

**Title (locked):** *The Third Option: How to Choose, Build, and Own a Life That Fits*

**Type:** Prescriptive method book. Not a memoir, not a research publication. Story and examples thread through as evidence; the method is the structure. Atomic Habits shape, not a life story.

**Length:** ~50,000 words. Floor 45k, ceiling 60k.

**Proof:** One or two curated outside anchors per chapter, then the method carries it. Not academic apparatus, not zero.

**Anti-balloon rule:** Several books' worth of material exists. This book states the method cleanly and proves it works. If a story doesn't prove a mechanism, it belongs in a later branch.

**Publishing:** Self-publish. KDP plus IngramSpark for print reach. Traditional is too slow and takes rights and control, which kills the claim-the-language goal. Do not cut corners on three things: developmental and copy editing, cover design, interior formatting. *(Verify current KDP and IngramSpark specs at production time; those terms shift.)*

**Strategic purpose (v1.1):** the book is the credibility anchor for Jena's conference speaking. It is the trunk of the brand, not a standalone product. Claim-the-language and voice consistency carry extra weight because of this.

## 4. Structure (Outline)

Intro + three parts of three chapters + a closing loop chapter + a worksheet appendix. Roughly eleven pieces at ~4,500 words.

- **Introduction · The Third Option**: the two doors, the third door, the persistence-over-perfection promise, the method in one breath.
- **Part One · CHOOSE (Strategy):** 1. Break the Cycle · 2. Know Your Core · 3. Choose What Fits
- **Part Two · BUILD (Creativity):** 4. Start Before You're Ready · 5. Dig Into What's Yours · 6. The Messy Middle
- **Part Three · OWN (Operations):** 7. Own What You Built · 8. Build the Systems That Keep It · 9. Keep Bettering It
- **Closing · Run the Loop**: 10. Choose, Build, Own on repeat; a life that fits is maintained, not achieved.
- **Appendix · The Fit Audit**: the self-assessment readers keep.

*Chapter 6, the Messy Middle, is where the thesis lives (persistence, imperfection as the cost of building). Protect it.*
*Part One is expanded to beat level in a separate sheet (not yet in this repo). Build and Own get the same treatment once the grain is confirmed.*

## 5. Design System

**Palette (working tokens, tune to the final photo):**

| Role | Color | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Primary / type / structure | Plum | `#5C1A3B` |
| Accent / energy | Bubblegum | `#FF7FB0` |
| Ground / "paper" | Cream | `#FBF6F0` |
| Fine text | Ink | `#2A1220` |

Discipline: roughly 60 plum / 10 bubblegum / 30 cream. Two brights need a neutral to read editorial instead of candy. Spend the boldness once per frame.

**Type pairing (locked as working choice):**
- **Display / title:** Fraunces. High-contrast editorial serif with warmth. Carries femininity and gravitas at once.
- **Text / labels:** Archivo. Clean structured grotesque. The discipline under the serif.
- *Both are placeholders a designer can swap for licensed cousins.*

**Icon system:** each stage gets one line-style icon on a single visual logic (open → assembling → closed): Choose is a path that branches and picks one, Build is shapes interlocking, Own is a closed loop. Single stroke weight, soft rounded terminals.

**Master method mark:** the three stage icons interlock into one emblem, three arcs closing into a ring, "a life that fits" made visual. Same system at two scales: the stage icons live inside the book, the combined mark becomes the spine, avatar, and slide stamp.

**Two registers:** the cover and method mark live in the credible register (editorial, restrained). The playful, personality-forward register (bubblegum energy, any lipstick or heels) lives in the campaign, author brand, and social. Same DNA, more play, so credibility never gets spent on the wrong asset.

## 6. Cover Direction

**Concept:** Faceless and embodied. Body, not face. The suit, the stance, and the hammer say everything, and it stays universal and reshootable. The method worn, not illustrated: a sharp suit plus a hammer equals a woman who builds.

**Current working cover (from the AI test Jena liked):** crisp white tailored pantsuit over a plum silk cami, both hands holding a claw hammer with a plum-wrapped handle, glossy plum manicure, on a solid bubblegum-pink background. Title "The Third Option" in Fraunces, title case, plum, lower third. Subtitle and byline in plum. Plum repeats four times (cami, handle, nails, title), which is what makes it read designed, not decorated.

**Open issue · thumbnail contrast:** plum type on bubblegum pink is lower-contrast than it looks at full size. Test at Amazon-thumbnail size. If the top line goes muddy, fixes are: make the title white, seat the whole title over the white of the legs, or deepen the plum. Covers are won at thumbnail size.

**Signature identifier (in progress):** two answers so far. An everywhere signature that can be a bold bubblegum lip in face-forward assets, and a cover signature below the neck, the bubblegum manicure on the hammer hands. On a pink ground the accent inverts to plum, so this cover's real identity is the pink-and-plum *pairing*. Decide on purpose whether the one thing is the bubblegum, the plum, or the two together, and make sure it survives on a white background too.

**Personality note:** the grip can read a little still. A slight hip shift or a one-handed rest adds the personality. If the contrast fix isn't enough, a shoulders-to-thigh crop buys cleaner title space.

## 7. People & Naming Policy (v1.1)

- **Kecia** (the friend at swim lessons, source of the opportunity advice) and **Michelle** (the mentor who reframed Jena's wiring as superpowers): fine to name at the level of common practice. Standard treatment is first name plus a light intro ("a friend at my son's swim lessons," "a mentor named Michelle"), with a personal heads-up to each before publication. "Mentor" as a label is an approved fallback. Decide per scene, not globally.
- **The gym story** (age 16, coaching at one gym while competing at another): fine to tell. Keep identifying details at the level the story needs and no further.
- Full names and organizations only if the book ever quotes or borrows credibility directly, which it currently doesn't need.
